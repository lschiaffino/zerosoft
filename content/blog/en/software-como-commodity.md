In 1900 a serious factory ran its own power plant: a room of dynamos, dedicated engineers, constant maintenance. A better generator meant more output.

Thirty years later the grid had arrived, electricity was priced close to the cost of producing it, and no factory talked about its generator again. Work software is following the same path.

## What happens when a product commoditizes

It tends to happen in three steps.

**The price falls toward marginal cost.** Electricity fell to the cost of fuel and transmission. Software falls to the cost of running servers, which is public and billed per month, not per user. $50 per user per month to keep contacts in a table only holds while customers think something scarce is inside.

**Differentiation moves.** When products are interchangeable, the winner is whoever distributes best. In software it moves to what can't be copied: trust, service and the data you already have in the tool.

**Incumbents talk about everything except cost.** "Value", "experience", "ecosystem". Rarely what it costs to run.

## Your CRM is plumbing

Nobody chooses a hotel for its generator. They choose it for what the hotel does with the power.

Your CRM works the same way. Your competitor has the same one, and the same link shortener. What sets you apart is what you build on top: the product, the customer relationships, the speed. Money overpaid for plumbing doesn't go there.

## What users get

- **Prices tied to usage.** No per-seat licenses and no renewal hikes.
- **Boring tools.** Like electricity: it's there and it works.
- **Budget for what matters.** What goes to undifferentiated subscriptions today can go to what differentiates you.

That's the shape of zerosoft: open code you can self-host for $0, or zerosoft Cloud with a free tier and usage billing per workspace (clicks, bookings, emails, storage), with free seats.
