Miro publishes a [guide to the seven stages of the customer journey](https://miro.com/customer-journey-map/what-are-7-steps-map-customer-journey) and a gallery of examples to draw on its canvas. It helps you understand the format. What it rarely covers is the following Monday: the map looks nice on a board and nobody changes anything.

This post walks a small team through the whole loop: build the map from what you already know about your customers, run a one-hour session and finish with assigned tasks. We use the Customer journey template that ships with Board, the zerosoft whiteboard.

## What the template gives you

When you create a new board you can pick "Customer journey". Board lays out five stage columns: Discover, Consider, Buy, Use and Recommend. Three rows run across them, each with stickies of its own color:

- **Actions** (yellow): what the person does at that stage.
- **Feelings** (red): what they feel.
- **Opportunities** (green): what we could change.

That is fifteen empty stickies ready to fill. Stages are editable like any text on the canvas, so if your business has no "Buy" step (a service with a trial, say), rename it to "Trial" and carry on.

## Before the session: pick one person and one stretch

A map that tries to cover every customer comes out generic. Pick a single persona named by role, for example "owner of a small shop who asks for support over WhatsApp", and a narrow stretch: from first hearing about you to paying the first invoice, or from paying to renewing.

Then gather real material. With it the session fits in an hour; without it you end up trading opinions:

1. Five recent support or sales conversations. If you use Chat, keep it open in another tab and copy the exact phrases.
2. The reasons the last few deals were lost, if you wrote them down.
3. The three questions people asked most often before buying.

Paste the phrases as they are into loose stickies outside the template. This is raw material, and you sort it during the session.

## A 60-minute session

One person drives the canvas and the rest watch or edit. Board shows who has the board open and their cursors, and the board has a shared timer (Workshop menu) that everyone sees. Each block gets its own clock.

**Minutes 0 to 5: framing.** Write the persona and the stretch at the top. If someone suggests adding another customer type, park it in a corner for a different map.

**Minutes 5 to 25: actions.** Timer at 20 minutes. Place the phrases from your material in the yellow row, one per stage or more. If a stage ends up empty, don't invent anything: mark it with a "we don't know" note. Those notes are the most valuable output of the session, because they show where you still need to talk to customers.

**Minutes 25 to 40: feelings.** For each stage, one red sticky with what the person felt, in their words: "I'm not sure this includes setup", "I'm nervous about entering my card". If you have no quote, leave the note empty instead of guessing.

**Minutes 40 to 55: opportunities.** Everyone writes concrete changes on green stickies. A concrete change has a verb and an object: "show a price on the home page", not "improve transparency". You can paste the whole list at once with the Stickies paste tool: one line per sticky, and words with # become tags (for example `#pricing`, `#onboarding`).

**Minutes 55 to 60: voting.**

## Voting without bias

Open a dot vote from Workshop. You choose how many votes each person gets (with ten opportunities, three each is enough) and everyone spends theirs with the + button on the stickies. Votes stay hidden until you close the vote, so nobody follows what the boss picked. When you close it, you see the results per sticky.

## From stickies to tasks

This is the step most maps skip. Select the two or three most voted green stickies, open the Track button and choose the project. Board creates one issue per sticky with a link back to the board, and shows the issue status below each sticky. Stickies that already have an issue are not duplicated.

Now the map has visible consequences: when someone opens the board a month later, every chosen opportunity shows whether it is done.

## Agenda template to copy

Paste it into a Docs page or the meeting description:

- Persona and stretch: ______
- Material: 5 conversations, lost-deal reasons, frequent questions
- 0:00 Framing (5 min)
- 0:05 Actions (20 min)
- 0:25 Feelings (15 min)
- 0:40 Opportunities (15 min)
- 0:55 Voting and selection (5 min)
- Afterwards: create Track issues and set a date to review the map

## Sharing it with people who weren't there

To show the result to someone outside the workspace, Board generates a read-only link that you can turn on and off whenever you want. For a meeting with leadership, build frames for the stages and use Present mode: you move through them like slides and can export the frames to PDF.

## What this version is missing

Board does not import data from your other apps into the map today. Quotes from Chat or loss reasons from the CRM are copied by hand. There is also no template that calculates anything for you: the map is a whiteboard, not a report. In exchange, the board belongs to your team: you can self-host it with the rest of the suite without paying per seat, and the issues that come out of the map live in the same system as the rest of your work.

## When to review it

Put a date in the calendar, two or three months out. Reopen the board and look at which opportunities ended up Done and which feelings changed. If the red row looks the same, the change missed the real problem, and it is worth talking to customers again before adding more tasks.
