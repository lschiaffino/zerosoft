Miro explains the [priority matrix](https://miro.com/strategic-planning/what-is-a-priority-matrix/) as a way to rank initiatives by their value and what they cost to do, and offers several templates to build one. The idea is old and good. The real problem for a small team is different: the backlog has thirty items, there is time for four, and everyone defends their own.

This guide is for running that conversation in Board, the zerosoft whiteboard. A matrix template is included, so the drawing part is done. What matters is how to place the cards without the loudest voice winning.

## What each quadrant means

Board's **Priority matrix** template draws four quadrants. The highest-impact items go at the top and the lowest-effort ones on the left:

- **Quick wins** (high impact, low effort): done first.
- **Big bets** (high impact, high effort): you pick one or two and plan them.
- **Fill-ins** (low impact, low effort): done when there is a spare hour.
- **Money pits** (low impact, high effort): dropped, and it helps to write down why.

Each quadrant comes with a sample sticky. Delete them or replace them with the first real item.

## Step 1: write down what impact and effort mean

Before placing a single card, the team agrees on two scales. Without that, "high impact" means different things to sales and to engineering. Write the definitions in a text box beside the matrix. An example for a small SaaS product:

| | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | Fewer than 5 customers would notice | Moves the quarter's metric (signups, retention or revenue) |
| Effort | One person, up to 2 days | More than one person or more than 2 weeks |

The figures are examples: pick your own, but make them numbers, not adjectives. If the team cannot agree on this table, you have already learned something the matrix would not have told you.

## Step 2: load the list without debating it

Build the candidate list in a note or spreadsheet, one item per line. In Board open **Stickies** and use **Paste a list**: it creates one sticky per line, up to 100 per paste. Words starting with `#` become tags, up to 8 per sticky. A line like `Import contacts from CSV #crm` becomes a sticky with the tag `crm`.

Each sticky takes up to 500 characters, but write little: a verb and an object. "Rewrite onboarding" is useless. "Remove the domain verification step from onboarding" can be estimated.

If your backlog already lives in a spreadsheet or another board, the Stickies panel also imports a CSV with a text column, including the one Miro exports.

## Step 3: place in silence, with hidden votes

The classic mistake is debating item by item while someone drags. With ten items you are an hour in. Do it in two passes.

**Individual pass (10 minutes).** Turn on Board's private mode. Each person creates their stickies directly in the quadrant where they think each one belongs (the list from step 2 stays beside the matrix as a reference, and everyone places one card per item). What they write in that mode stays as their own draft: the others only see that cards exist, covered, so nobody is swayed by the item the boss already placed. When everyone is done, the facilitator reveals and the server adds the drafts to the board in one go. Use the **Workshop** timer to stop at ten minutes.

**Group pass (20 minutes).** With everything revealed, first look at the stickies many people put in the same place: nothing to discuss there. Spend the time on the ones that landed in different quadrants. That gap is almost always a fact someone has and the others do not ("that touches billing, it's two weeks, not two days").

This pass has live editing with cursors, so several people can move cards at once. Even so, it works better when one person drives the board while the rest talk: a discussion goes better when everyone watches a single cursor.

## Step 4: break ties with dots

You will probably end up with seven or eight stickies in **Quick wins** and no room for all of them. In **Workshop**, open a **dot vote** and set the votes per person between 1 and 20. A reasonable rule: half the items in the quadrant, with a minimum of three. Votes stay hidden until you close the vote, and then the totals per sticky appear, highest first.

For Big bets, voting works differently: pick only one. One big bet per quarter is what a five-person team can sustain.

## Step 5: from matrix to work

A matrix that stays on the board gets forgotten. Select the winning stickies, click the **Track** button in the toolbar and pick the project. Board creates one issue per sticky, with the card's text and a link back to the board, and a sticky that already has an issue is not duplicated. After that, the issue's status and assignee show under the sticky, so the matrix becomes a map of what is moving.

For the rejected ones, change their color to gray and leave a line with the reason. In three months someone will propose the same thing, and the answer will already be written.

## A 45-minute agenda template

| Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Read the definitions of impact and effort |
| 5 to 15 | Individual placement in private mode |
| 15 to 20 | Reveal and look at where people agreed |
| 20 to 35 | Debate only the items with different placements |
| 35 to 40 | Dot vote on Quick wins |
| 40 to 45 | Issues in Track and a date for the next review |

Repeat the matrix every quarter: duplicate the board, move what is still valid and delete what is done.

## Two warnings

A two-axis matrix compresses a lot. It ignores risk, dependencies and the fact that some things are mandatory (a certificate renewal will never win on impact). If something is mandatory, take it out of the matrix and schedule it directly.

And estimates are opinions. After each quarter, check how many quick wins ended up costing more than planned: that percentage, measured on your team, is worth more than any template.
