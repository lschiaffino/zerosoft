"Free" means two things in English: no charge, and no restrictions. The open source world has an old shorthand for it: free as in beer, free as in freedom. For a company choosing its tools, the second one is the one that lasts.

## A free plan is a price

A free plan belongs to the vendor. They can raise the price, shrink the plan, move a feature to the enterprise tier or shut the service down. Most teams have seen all four.

An open source license works the other way. The rights come with the code: you can read it, audit it, modify it and run it on any server, without asking anyone. A vendor can stop maintaining it, but they can't take it back.

## Why open projects tend to last

Proprietary products answer to whoever funds them. Open projects answer to whoever uses them, and if a project goes the wrong way, someone forks it. Linux, Postgres, Redis and nginx all run most of the internet's infrastructure. The tools office workers touch every day are the part that stayed closed.

## Leaving should be easy

The usual SaaS pattern: easy to start, comfortable to stay, hard to leave. Data goes in through a nice form and comes out as a partial CSV.

With the code under AGPLv3, leaving zerosoft Cloud means taking your data and running the same software on your own server. We designed it that way on purpose. If leaving is easy, we have to stay good enough that you don't.

## How it's paid for

The software costs nothing to use. Servers do. If you want us to run it, zerosoft Cloud has a free tier and then charges per workspace for usage: clicks, bookings, emails and storage. Seats are free. Self-hosting costs $0.

A free plan is a promise. A license is a right. For tools your company depends on, pick the right.
